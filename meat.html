The Meat

Know the cut

This is not a menu. Every cut is listed with the way it is trimmed, the wood it sees, the temperature it is held at and the hours it takes, because understanding the cook is most of the pleasure of eating it.

Whole packer, flat and point

Brisket

The cut the whole method was built around. Trimmed by hand, seasoned with nothing but coarse salt and pepper, and left alone until it gives.

  • Smoking time12–14 hours
  • WoodOak with a little hickory
  • Temperature110°C chamber, 96°C internal
  • FlavourDeep, beefy, peppery bark with rendered fat under it
  • ServedSliced pencil-thick against the grain, butcher paper, pickles
Plate ribs, three bones

Short Ribs

Beef ribs are what brisket wants to be when it grows up. Heavy marbling, heavy bone, and a bark you can hear when the knife goes in.

  • Smoking time7–8 hours
  • WoodOak
  • Temperature115°C chamber, 98°C internal
  • FlavourRich and buttery, heavy bark, almost no chew left
  • ServedOne bone per person, cut between the bones, served hot
Back ribs from the rib primal

Beef Back Ribs

Less meat than plate ribs and more of it between the bones, which makes them the best thing on the smoker to eat with your hands.

  • Smoking time5 hours
  • WoodOak and cherry
  • Temperature115°C chamber
  • FlavourLeaner than plate ribs, sweeter edge from the cherry
  • ServedRacked whole, sliced at the table
Chuck roll

Pulled Beef

Chuck has more connective tissue than brisket and turns into strands rather than slices. This is the cut for sandwiches and for feeding a crowd.

  • Smoking time9 hours
  • WoodOak
  • Temperature110°C chamber, 97°C internal
  • FlavourSoft, moist, heavily seasoned, holds sauce well
  • ServedPiled into brioche with slaw, or on rice
Whole bird, brined overnight

Smoked Chicken

Poultry cannot take twelve hours of oak, so it gets lighter wood and a shorter cook. The overnight brine is what keeps the breast from drying.

  • Smoking time3 hours
  • WoodCitrus and olive wood
  • Temperature135°C chamber, finished hot for the skin
  • FlavourLight smoke, salty brine, crisp skin, juicy breast
  • ServedQuartered, with pickles and a vinegar sauce
Coarse ground beef, house blend

House Sausages

Ground and stuffed in house on the morning of the cook, then hung in the chamber. The snap of the casing is the whole point.

  • Smoking time2.5 hours
  • WoodHickory
  • Temperature115°C chamber, 74°C internal
  • FlavourSharp, spiced, snappy casing, smoke all the way through
  • ServedTwo links per portion, mustard on the side
Bone-in shoulder

Lamb Shoulder

The one cut on the rack that belongs to this region before it belongs to Texas. Olive wood keeps the smoke close to what a Jordanian table already knows.

  • Smoking time8 hours
  • WoodOlive wood
  • Temperature115°C chamber, 95°C internal
  • FlavourLocal and familiar, olive smoke instead of oak, deeply savoury
  • ServedPulled, with flatbread and yoghurt
Boneless breast, brined

Smoked Turkey Breast

Proof that smoke does not have to mean heavy. Brined for a day, smoked over cherry, and sliced thin.

  • Smoking time4 hours
  • WoodCherry
  • Temperature120°C chamber, 74°C internal
  • FlavourMild, clean, gently sweet
  • ServedCold-sliced for sandwiches or warm with sides

Sides & Sauces

What sits beside it

Sides are built to cut through fat, not to compete with it. Sauce always arrives on the side.

House pickles

Cucumber and green chilli, sharp enough to reset the palate between slices.

Charred corn

Grilled over the firebox coals, dressed with lemon and sumac.

Smoked beans

Cooked under the brisket rack all day, catching the drippings.

Two sauces

A thin vinegar sauce for poultry, and a dark peppery one for beef.
